01 / PURPOSE & RESPONSIBILITIES

What this system does

A staff-operated logistics workspace. The customer supplies the address and parcel details; the company manages the booking.

The central business rule

Use the company’s own network first.
Staff enters the pickup and delivery addresses. The system checks own-company branch coverage. If the route is covered, staff books through own logistics. If it is not covered, staff compares available external courier services and books the selected service.

Two separate choices — keep them clear

ChoiceOptionsMeaning
Shipment categoryDomestic / InternationalWhere the parcel travels: within India or across a national border.
Fulfilment methodOwn logistics / External courierWho transports the parcel: the company’s own branch network or a courier partner.

Domestic does not automatically mean own logistics. International does not automatically mean an external courier. Both categories can use either method, depending on the company’s coverage.

Who is responsible?

Person / systemResponsibility
CustomerProvides sender/receiver information, destination address and parcel details. No customer portal is included in this blueprint.
Company operations staffCreates the shipment, checks coverage, selects a service, records freight collection and manages the shipment.
Origin / destination branch staffHandles own-network loading, dispatch, unloading and delivery confirmations.
Company owner / adminSets up branches, staff permissions, courier connections and business settings.
Courier integrationIn the production design: returns serviceability/rates, confirms bookings and provides tracking and documents.
Company context applies throughout
Branches, staff, shipments, quotes, financial records and reports must belong to the signed-in company. Role and branch permissions must be enforced by the backend in production.

How to read this document

Pages 3–5 explain navigation and the coverage decision. Pages 6–9 describe all four shipping journeys. Pages 10–12 cover confirmation, delivery and exceptions. Pages 13–14 cover supporting screens, sample walkthroughs and implementation boundaries.

04 / COMMON BOOKING STEPS

Capture the shipment. Check coverage.

The initial pages are shared by domestic and international shipments.

Step 1 · Choose the shipment category

Domestic: pickup and delivery within India. International: pickup in India and delivery in another country in this prototype. The current prototype does not model inbound imports or foreign-to-foreign shipments.

Step 2 · Enter addresses and parcel information

SectionInformation captured
PickupSender name, contact number, country, city, PIN code and full street / building / area address.
DeliveryReceiver name, contact number, destination country, city, PIN / postal code, full address and email. Email is required for international bookings in the demo.
ParcelContents description, package quantity, weight and dimensions per package, total declared goods value, currency and handling notes.

The demo uses identical packages in a booking. Mixed package sizes and multiple parcel rows should be added when the production requirements are defined.

Step 3 · Evaluate own-company coverage

Pickup covered?Delivery covered?Route result
YesYes — different served branchOwn logistics service.
YesNoExternal courier comparison.
NoYes or NoExternal courier comparison, provided a partner serves both addresses.
Same branch at both endsYesDemo sends this to external comparison. Production local-delivery rules remain to be decided.
Coverage must mean service to the address
A branch existing in the same city is not sufficient evidence of production serviceability. Actual branch delivery areas, PIN/postal coverage and pickup capability must be defined. The prototype uses country + city + postal prefix as a simplified demonstration.

What the coverage result page shows

  • Matched origin and destination branches, if found.
  • A clear reason when pickup or delivery is outside the own network.
  • Exactly one primary next path: own service or external courier comparison.
  • An Edit addresses button to correct the shipment before continuing.

05 / JOURNEY A

Domestic → Own logistics

Example: Hyderabad → Vijayawada, where both addresses are served by the company’s own branches.

  1. Dashboard → New shipment → Domestic. Staff starts a shipment within India.
  2. Addresses & parcel page. Enter sender, receiver, addresses, package information and declared value.
  3. Check branch coverage. Origin matches Hyderabad Central; destination matches Vijayawada Hub.
  4. Coverage result → View own logistics service. Show the own-company branch route, service description, charges and estimated delivery.
  5. Select own service → Continue to review. No international customs page is shown.
  6. Review & freight collection. Staff checks all details and selects Paid, To Pay, Credit or FOC.
  7. Confirm booking. Create a shipment and show the shipment ID, GRN and LR number; allow receipt download.
  8. Open Own logistics. Origin staff loads the parcel, then records dispatch.
  9. Destination receipt. Receiving staff confirms unloading at the destination branch.
  10. Final delivery. Staff records receiver confirmation and any freight collection, then marks the shipment Delivered.
Booking success / GRN + LR
↓
Loading queue → Vehicle & driver details → Confirm loading
↓
Dispatch queue → Dispatch reference → Confirm dispatch
↓
Unloading queue → Receiving staff details → Confirm unloading
↓
Delivery queue → Receiver + collection → Complete delivery
Customer delivery remains part of the journey
The own-network shipment is not finished simply because it reaches a branch. Delivery must be confirmed using the company’s agreed branch collection / final-mile delivery arrangement.
Optional onward branch transfer — production extension
The existing backend has branch-to-branch operations. If intermediate transfers are required in the new design, the onward branch and final destination must be explicit: unload → onward load → next branch receipt → final delivery. This optional multi-leg routing is not a separate interactive journey in the current dummy prototype.

06 / JOURNEY B

Domestic → External courier

Example: Hyderabad → Jaipur, where the own company network does not cover the destination address.

  1. Dashboard → New shipment → Domestic. Staff enters addresses and parcel information.
  2. Check branch coverage. Show that the destination is outside the own branch service area.
  3. Coverage result → Compare courier services. Use pickup PIN, delivery PIN, package weight/dimensions and required service information.
  4. Compare the returned services. Show service availability, provider, standard/express option, total quote, charge breakdown and delivery estimate.
  5. Sort and select. Staff can choose Lowest price or Fastest delivery. The cheapest service is a suggestion; staff makes the selection.
  6. Continue to review. Check addresses, selected courier, parcel details and customer freight collection.
  7. Confirm booking. In production, send one booking request to the selected courier and wait for confirmation.
  8. Booking success. Show the company shipment ID, courier name, AWB/tracking number, available label and pickup state.
  9. Pickup and tracking. Follow courier updates through final delivery at the receiver address.

How the service comparison should read

Visible informationWhy staff needs it
Serviceable pickup + deliveryOnly choose a partner that can handle the complete route.
Total quote + charge breakdownCompare the actual payable shipping amount, including disclosed surcharges.
Estimated delivery + service typeBalance price against speed and handling requirements.
Pickup / drop-off availabilityUnderstand how the parcel enters the courier network.
External means a complete partner-handled shipment
This blueprint’s primary external path uses the partner from pickup through delivery. It does not silently combine an own first leg with an external final leg. Hybrid transfers need a separately designed handover flow.
Current demo behaviour
RoadLink and SwiftParcel are fictional domestic services. Quotes, confirmation, labels and tracking are simulated. The demo does not call a courier API or request a real pickup.

07 / JOURNEY C

International → Own logistics

Example: Hyderabad → Dubai, using an own overseas branch in the illustrative company network.

  1. Dashboard → New shipment → International. Staff chooses an overseas destination.
  2. Addresses & parcel page. Capture pickup in India, foreign destination country/address, receiver contact/email, contents, dimensions and value/currency.
  3. Check own branch coverage. Confirm the company can serve the origin and the overseas destination address.
  4. View own overseas service. Show origin branch → overseas branch, shipping charges and estimated transit time.
  5. Select service → Customs & documents. International paperwork is required in the workflow even when both branches belong to the company.
  6. Complete the declaration. Enter shipment purpose, item description, HS code, invoice reference, declared value and duties responsibility.
  7. Add supporting documents. Attach the required invoice and sender KYC / identity documents in the demo. Actual requirements must be configurable by route and service.
  8. Review & confirm. Verify the route, recipient address, customs details, documents, charges and collection instructions.
  9. Booking success. Show shipment ID and own-network references. Continue to the company’s operational shipment journey.
  10. Dispatch → Overseas receipt → Delivery. Track the international movement, receive at the destination branch and confirm final delivery.

International information in the booking record

Data groupSaved information
DestinationCountry, full address, postal code if applicable, receiver phone and email.
Goods declarationContents, purpose, item classification reference, invoice, value and currency.
DocumentsRequired document references and validation state.
Charges & dutiesShipping quote separately from any customs duties / destination taxes; responsible party recorded.
Own overseas does not remove international processing
The production design needs the company’s actual export handling, clearance and destination delivery process. Any international partner handovers and clearance milestones must be added explicitly; the dummy screens do not prove operational or regulatory readiness.
Demo address detail
Dubai Overseas is a fictional own branch. The postal code 00000 is only a demo placeholder, not a production postal-address rule.

08 / JOURNEY D

International → External courier

Example: Hyderabad → London, where the company has no own branch coverage for the destination address.

  1. New shipment → International. Staff enters pickup and overseas delivery details, parcel contents, weight/dimensions and value.
  2. Check branch coverage. Show the unavailable own route and explain the destination coverage gap.
  3. Compare international services. Request serviceability and rates for the destination country/postal area and the full parcel details.
  4. Select the service. Compare economy/express, total shipping quote, estimated transit time and disclosed exclusions.
  5. Customs & documents. Complete the declaration and required paperwork before booking confirmation.
  6. Review charges and duties responsibility. Make clear which amounts are included, excluded, estimated or payable later.
  7. Confirm booking. In production, submit validated shipment and document references to the selected provider.
  8. Show courier confirmation. Record shipment ID, provider booking reference, AWB, label, available invoice/customs documents and pickup state.
  9. Pickup → International movement → Delivery. Show courier milestones, including clearance/exception events when the provider supplies them.

The international rate page must be explicit

  • Rates are comparable only when they describe the same parcel, route and included services.
  • The demo shows shipping prices in INR. Declared goods currency is a separate field.
  • Customs duties and destination taxes are excluded from the demo quote.
  • Recording “Sender” or “Recipient” as duties payer is an instruction, not proof of a provider-supported duties-paid service.
  • Production must validate the chosen duties arrangement against the selected carrier/service.
Required documents before confirming
The prototype blocks progression when invoice and sender identity document filenames are missing. “Use sample documents” adds demo filenames only. No real document is uploaded or reviewed.
Current demo behaviour
WorldBridge and GlobalAir are fictional international providers. Their rates, transit estimates, booking confirmation and tracking events are illustrative.

09 / REVIEW & CONFIRMATION

Check the booking before creating it

The review page joins the selected route, parcel, documents and collection instructions in one place.

Review page checklist

  • Shipment: Domestic / International and Own logistics / External courier.
  • Addresses: sender, receiver, complete pickup/delivery addresses, contact numbers and international email.
  • Parcel: contents, quantity, weights, dimensions, declared goods value and currency.
  • Service: provider or own branches, service type, estimated delivery and charge breakdown.
  • International: customs declaration, invoice, document references and duties responsibility.
  • Collection: freight payment type, collection/account method and credit party when required.

Freight collection choices in the prototype

Payment typeStaff instructionAvailable path
PaidRecord freight as paid at booking. Demo methods: Cash, UPI or company account.Own + External
To PayCollect the freight at delivery. Demo requires the full expected amount before own delivery completion.Own only
CreditRequire a credit party/account name and record the receivable arrangement.Own only
FOCCustomer freight is zero; retain the illustrative internal shipping cost.Own only
Separate three kinds of money
Customer freight collection is different from company settlement with a courier, and both are different from the CargoNest platform subscription. The prototype records collection instructions; it does not process payments.

From booking to final delivery

Own-network actions are performed by staff. Courier movements are received from the partner in production.

Own logistics operational pages

Queue / current stateStaff inputAction and next state

Dispatch / LoadedDispatch reference and remarks.Confirm dispatch → In transit.
Unloading / In transitReceiving staff, destination receipt and remarks.Confirm unloading → Unloaded.
Delivery / UnloadedReceiver name, contact, freight collected and remarks.Complete delivery → Delivered.

The demo lets one staff user explore both origin and destination actions. Production must restrict each operation to the assigned role, company, branch and eligible shipment state.

External courier tracking

Booked → Pickup scheduled → Picked up
↓
In transit → Out for delivery → Delivered

Production tracking should also preserve provider-specific details such as customs clearance, delivery attempts, delays and proof of delivery when available. These events do not need to become separate mandatory booking pages.

Shipment details page

  • Shows the timeline with current stage, event date/time and responsible staff/provider.
  • Shows addresses, parcel details, selected service, quote breakdown and freight type.
  • Shows own route references or the courier AWB and document actions.
  • Shows international declaration and document references for overseas shipments.
  • Offers only actions valid for the shipment’s current state.

How staff finds a shipment

All shipments: filter by category, fulfilment or status, and search ID, city, receiver or courier details. Tracking: enter a shipment ID, GRN, LR or AWB and open its details.

Demo and existing backend status names differ
The prototype shows dispatch/In transit as a separate own-network step. The existing backend uses numeric stages: 0 Booked, 1 Loaded, 2 Unloaded, 3 Missing, 4 Delivered, 5 Cancelled. Define the production state mapping before connecting these screens.

11 / EXCEPTIONS & SAFEGUARDS

When the normal path cannot continue

Keep the staff informed and prevent invalid, duplicate or misleading bookings.

SituationExpected product behaviourCurrent prototype
Missing / invalid detailsHighlight invalid fields; retain entered information; do not proceed.Required fields, number ranges and basic input formats are checked.
Own network lacks coverageExplain the gap and continue to external services.Implemented using demo branch matching.
No external service availableShow no service; allow address/details correction or an alternative arrangement.Production requirement; dummy partners always return sample services on the external path.
International documents missingPrevent final review/confirmation until required details are present.Invoice + identity filenames required.

Missing, damaged, delayed or returned parcelRecord exception, owner, resolution and any return/refund workflow.Separate production design; not a complete demo workflow.

Production safeguards to preserve the intended flow

  • Enforce company, role and branch permissions on the server, including updates and deletes.
  • Use allowed state transitions; prevent generic edits from bypassing loading or delivery rules.
  • Keep the shipment, operation and financial updates consistent when a request fails.
  • Make carrier booking and payment reconciliation safe to retry; deduplicate provider events.
  • Treat quote totals, actual freight collected, refunds and subscription revenue separately.
Cancellation issue found during source review
The existing cancelBooking handler currently reports success without setting bookingStatus to 5. Fix and verify this before using that endpoint for the proposed cancellation behaviour.

What the working dummy prototype includes

Clickable pages for all four journeys; simplified coverage; service selection and rate sorting; international document requirements; booking confirmation; own logistics queues; simulated partner tracking; search and filters; cancellation before movement; demo HTML documents; reports/CSV; local browser persistence and reset.

What still needs production implementation

  • Coverage: address-level branch serviceability, local delivery, final-mile and any mixed own/courier handovers.
  • Providers: actual selected courier APIs, normalized quotes, real booking, pickup, tracking, labels and failure reconciliation.
  • International: country/service-dependent documents, actual overseas operations and provider-supported duties arrangements.
  • Money: own tariffs, final tax/fee rules, customer collection, courier settlement, credit ledger, cancellation/refunds.
  • Access & data: server permissions, state mapping, audit history, uploads, transactional writes and existing-data compatibility.

Terms used in this document

GRN / LR: own-company parcel/consignment references. AWB: courier shipment tracking reference. Serviceability: whether a network can serve the entered addresses. FOC: free of customer freight charge. HS code: goods classification reference used in the international declaration.
